# SMART — Role Assessment Blueprint: MBA — Business Analytics
### Domain → Sub-Domain → Concept, weighted, mapped to L1–L5

**Grounding note:** Business Analytics is the single highest-demand MBA specialization in current 2026 hiring indices, cited with 28% year-over-year growth by NASSCOM/LinkedIn India hiring data — the strongest growth figure of any MBA track, ahead of Finance and Product Management.

**Calibration note:** concept-level weights are the calibration panel's first-pass judgment, to be validated against real regional hiring data.

---

## Level weightage summary

| Level | Composition |
|---|---|
| L1 | 40% general aptitude (stats/quant-weighted) + 25% Domain A + 35% Domain B |
| L2 | 30% Domain A (applied) + 25% Domain B (applied) + 30% Domain C + 15% Domain D |
| L3 | 100% Domain E |
| L4 | AI-assessed threshold model — artifact/case-work upload + adaptive simulation defense |
| L5 | 50% checklist + 30% practitioner judgment + 20% presentation |

---

## Domain A — Data Querying & Manipulation
*Justified by: the same skills-list overlap real IT-track data-analyst postings show — SQL and applied data-handling fluency are now named requirements in MBA-analytics postings, not just technical-track ones.*

**Sub-domain A1: SQL fundamentals**
| Concept | Weight | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Joins and aggregations | 30% | L1, L2 |
| Filtering and sorting logic | 25% | L1 |
| Basic subqueries | 25% | L2 |
| Query interpretation (reading, not just writing) | 20% | L1 |

**Sub-domain A2: Applied data handling**
| Concept | Weight | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Excel-based data manipulation | 35% | L2 |
| Data cleaning basics | 35% | L2 |
| Basic Python/pandas familiarity (where applicable) | 30% | L2 |

---

## Domain B — Statistical Reasoning & Interpretation
*Justified by: this is the domain that separates a candidate who can run an analysis from one who can be trusted to interpret it correctly — the single most consistently tested applied-judgment layer in analytics interviews.*

**Sub-domain B1: Descriptive & inferential statistics**
| Concept | Weight | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Central tendency and variance | 20% | L1 |
| Distribution and outlier reasoning | 20% | L1 |
| Correlation vs. causation | 25% | L1, L3 |
| Hypothesis-testing intuition | 20% | L1 |
| Sample-size and confidence caveats | 15% | L1, L3 |

**Sub-domain B2: Applied statistical judgment**
| Concept | Weight | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing the right statistical approach for a business question | 50% | L2 |
| Recognizing a flawed or misleading analysis | 50% | L2, L4 |

---

## Domain C — Business Intelligence & Visualization
*Justified by: dashboard and BI-tool fluency is the deliverable format most MBA-analytics work actually takes inside a real organization.*

**Sub-domain C1: Dashboard and reporting**
| Concept | Weight | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Building a dashboard around a business question | 40% | L2 |
| Chart-type selection judgment | 35% | L2 |
| Report structuring for a business audience | 25% | L2, L4 |

---

## Domain D — Applied Case: Dataset → Recommendation
*Justified by: the ability to move from raw data to a defensible business recommendation is what MBA-analytics roles are actually hired to do — not analysis for its own sake.*

**Sub-domain D1: Case-based application**
| Concept | Weight | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Structuring an open-ended business analytics case | 35% | L2 |
| Prioritizing which analysis actually answers the business question | 35% | L2, L4 |
| Framing a recommendation with clear next steps | 30% | L2, L4 |

---

## Domain E — Data Storytelling & Defense
*Justified by: translating an analysis into a story a non-technical executive will act on is the specific, real-interview skill that separates an analyst from a strategic hire.*

**Sub-domain E1: Explaining analytical decisions**
| Concept | Weight | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Explaining a statistical finding to a non-technical stakeholder | 35% | L3, L4 |
| Defending a methodology choice | 35% | L3, L4 |
| Naming the limitations of an analysis honestly | 30% | L3, L4 |

---

## Level 5 — Capstone
A real, anonymized business dataset, analyzed end-to-end into a recommendation deck — the same integrative structure as the IT-track Data Analyst capstone, adapted to a business-case framing. Scored 50% checklist / 30% practitioner judgment / 20% presentation.
